Lesson objective: I can write an evaluative paragraph using judgements, details and effects.

Build an evaluative paragraph

Plan four judgements about a film, link them with contrast, then finish with an overall verdict. Stuck? Use Hint or Model answer in each box.

Connect and judge

Name the type of viewer (families, action fans) and give a reason from your points. Then add “but it may not suit everyone”.
Overall, the film may suit families who enjoy fast action and humour, but it may not suit everyone.

Use a different point in each box.

Write your paragraph

Start from your notes, then rewrite in full sentences. Use “seems” and “may” to show these are your judgements, and link ideas with also, however, although and overall.

    Model paragraph

    Green = two positives. Blue = two negatives. Purple = contrast. Yellow = overall judgement.

    The action seems thrilling because Sonic’s speed turns every chase scene into a race, making viewers want to keep watching. Sonic also seems lovable because he is lonely yet cheeky, encouraging viewers to care about him. However, some jokes may be too childish because they rely on silly slapstick, which could bore older viewers. Although the fast pace creates excitement, rapid cuts may make some scenes harder to follow for viewers who prefer a slower story. Overall, the film may suit families who enjoy fast action and humour, but it may not suit everyone.

    • Positive 1: thrilling action → speedy chase scenes → excitement.
    • Positive 2: lovable character → lonely but cheeky → affection.
    • Negative 1: childish humour → silly slapstick → boredom for older viewers.
    • Negative 2: hard-to-follow scenes → rapid cuts → confusion for some viewers.

    “Seems” and “may” show these are your opinions, not facts. The second negative is a possible drawback of the same feature praised in the first positive: the pace that creates excitement can also confuse.
